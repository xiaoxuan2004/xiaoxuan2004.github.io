Research Interests

AI for Science

I am interested in how AI can accelerate the process of scientific discovery, from modeling complex biological systems to automating hypothesis generation and analysis. I am particularly drawn to virtual cells, computational models that simulate cellular states and responses.

Multi-omics Analysis & Algorithms

Selected Projects

01
Single-cell & Spatial Omics

Radiation and Aging: A Single-Cell and Spatial Transcriptomic Study

Previous studies have typically used single-cell analysis to investigate either aging or radiation mechanisms, with spatial transcriptomics serving as validation. However, although clinical evidence suggests a link between radiation exposure and aging, few studies have explored the relationship between them directly. We jointly analyze single-cell data and high-resolution spatial transcriptomic data collected across multiple time points and conditions, aiming to reveal how radiation and aging interact.

02
Multi-omics & Translational Research

Multi-omics and Dosimetry-Guided Radionuclide Therapy: From Animal Models to Clinical Translation

Existing studies of radionuclide therapy tend to rely on dosimetry alone and overlook how the treatment acts on the tissue microenvironment. By combining multi-omics with dosimetry, we aim to uncover the mechanisms behind the local efficacy of peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT). We treat the true tumor response as the joint outcome of physical dose and the microenvironment, and we study how it evolves from local dose distribution and omics changes to final clinical outcomes.

03
Machine Learning & Neuroimaging

Brain Age Estimation

Machine learning models have long been used to predict brain age accurately from neuroimaging data, and deviations from healthy aging patterns are often associated with accelerated brain aging and abnormalities. Accurate estimation of brain age therefore requires effective and precise diagnostic techniques, and many studies using different data-driven modeling approaches have contributed to this goal. We propose a novel algorithm that combines corpus data with existing brain aging datasets to further improve the accuracy of brain age estimation.

04
Multimodal Learning & Computer Vision

Cross-Modality Person Re-Identification

Person re-identification (ReID) aims to match the same individual across different cameras. Existing work mostly focuses on single-modality settings based on visible-light images, but at night, infrared images can provide useful information. The key challenge is how to fuse the two modalities and reduce the gap between them to achieve visible-infrared person re-identification. We propose a novel algorithm that combines Transformer and CNN architectures for efficient and accurate re-identification.
